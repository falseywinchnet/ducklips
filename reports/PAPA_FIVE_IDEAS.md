# Five physical hypotheses in the current tissue speech engine

The user requested five principled attempts to improve the sickly-sounding
current system, each saying Papa. Every take uses the same `Synthesizer`,
elastic-volume `TissueSource`, 29-coordinate `SomaticControl`, conservative
moving tongue and local airflow engine. No take selects the earlier two-mass
source or an earlier accepted Papa recording. These are independent hypotheses,
not a stack of five changes. A sixth freshly generated reference is retained
internally for comparison.

## Shared experiment

- Cue stream: `P AA0 P AA1`, generated by the existing utterance planner.
- Same reference body, 600 Pa breath intention, 110 Hz base pitch intention,
  speaking rate 0.8, seed 41, 48 kHz audio and 96 kHz tissue updates.
- Imperfect somatic holding remains active at noise fraction 0.08. There is
  no legacy jitter, shimmer or periodic tremor. Independent added aspiration
  and frication noise are zero for this comparison; physical pressure release
  and airway radiation remain active.
- Two planned vowels: 0.690893–0.792840 s and 0.889680–1.164830 s. Actual motion
  lags these intentions. Each complete file is 1.515 s; no audio is cropped,
  faded, spliced or equalized.
- Listening WAVs use a single constant gain per full take to put the peak at
  0.7 full scale. The exact gains are in each receipt. MP3s are encodings of
  those WAVs, not separately synthesized or processed voices.
- Each complete enhanced field has all 684 frequency rows, 128-sample time
  spacing and original magma color. Source-dependent display references
  compensate for the different constant listening gains. No companion STFT
  image or cropped/downsized enhanced panel is used.

One shared integration correction is necessary for the word test: the
planner's time-zero placements must establish initial body posture before
mechanical/acoustic history starts. The somatic controller now captures those
placements before the first physical step. This honors the initial closed-lip
/p/ and vowel posture. Later cues preserve accumulated history. A focused test
checks that the initial lip seal exists on the first block. All six fresh
renders contain this same correction.

## The five hypotheses

1. **Shared material relaxation.** The reference applies independent random
   relaxation clocks to Cholesky strain coordinates. These coordinates are
   algebraic channels, not independently identified biological material
   regions. This variant uses one fluctuating relaxation clock across the
   material, retaining its strength, mean time and randomness. Strain-history
   states remain independent and positive-rate dissipation is retained.
   Shared versus independent spatial fluctuation is a modeling hypothesis.

2. **Contact recovery damping.** The reference damps collision only while
   the contacting surface compresses. This variant also opposes recoil while
   tissue remains in contact. It uses the existing elastic contact law and
   viscosity, with total contact force clamped to be nonnegative so the folds
   cannot adhere. In both motion directions the additional force relative to
   the elastic law does nonpositive work. This is a test of the unloading
   law, not a softer contact parameter or an output smoothing filter.

3. **Energy-based strain hardening.** The reference body has linear bulk
   elasticity even at large deformation. This variant adds a positive quartic
   material energy, `E_material² / (2 E_scale)`, and derives the restoring
   force from it. `E_scale` uses the two-fold body volume, shear moduli and a
   prototype 15% strain scale. It tests whether growing restoring force can
   limit excessive excursions while permitting small motion. It is a reduced
   law, not a measured finite-strain fit; it also does not model all forms of
   nonlinear tissue behavior. Human large-amplitude shear measurements report
   intracycle stiffening as well as softening and thinning effects:
   [Chan, 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5959278/).

4. **Shared axial glottal hydraulics.** The reference tissue law computes
   slit resistance and a geometry-dependent jet area, while the tract inlet
   uses a different uniform aperture and omits that slit resistance. This
   variant carries the tissue passage's integrated resistance, axial air
   inertia and jet area into the existing local midpoint inlet exchange.
   Positive resistance enters that exchange directly. It retains one tract
   airflow state and requires no global solve. Tissue wall pressure still
   uses a quasi-steady approximation; this does not claim a complete unsteady
   fluid/tissue coupling. The importance of intraglottal pressure and inertive
   acoustic loading motivates this direction:
   [Titze, 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5677576/).

5. **Measured tissue posture feedback.** The reference maintains a reduced
   glottal posture coordinate, then moves the tissue equilibrium. This variant
   instead senses the actual area-weighted mean surface gap and applies an
   opposing-muscle holding force through the body's compiled posture direction.
   It reuses the 20 ms filtered sensing, 30 ms feedback delay and irregular
   twitch populations, with continuous interpolation of holding force between
   control samples. The equilibrium is left at its passive reference. The
   desired mean gap comes from the same glottal intention and convergent
   surface geometry. The fixed tissue attachments and assumed generalized
   muscle direction remain limitations; this is not an anatomical cartilage
   model or a pitch servo.

Structural vibration and acoustic loading can affect each other, so a more
organized output cannot be attributed to one parameter solely from its spectrum.
The continuum-model study of
[Zhang and colleagues, 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2748820/)
motivates testing these interactions rather than prescribing a vocal waveform.
The cited literature motivates physical questions; it does not validate these
five implementations or their parameter values.

## Checks and limits

The core synthesis, local airflow, somatic control and tissue-source test
suites pass. The additions check shared-clock stress dissipation, unforced
energy decay with contact recovery and strain hardening, positive axial-flow
coefficients, and energy/air-volume accounting with the added inlet resistance.
All six full Papa renders finish with zero reported geometry/airflow failures,
nonfinite samples or clipped samples. These checks do not establish naturalness.

The measured complete renders run about 5.6–5.9x realtime, excluding disk writes
and offline depiction. The 10x requirement is still unmet. The previously
identified representation issue remains audio-rate recomputation of tissue
and airway coefficients; performance optimization is parked while these
sound-quality experiments are compared. No variant is declared a winner
without the user's listening assessment.

## Visual review

The first three full fields all retain the two voiced syllable regions and
the intermediate closure. Shared relaxation leaves substantial broadband
texture. Recoil damping and strain hardening show more organized low bands
in the second vowel, with strain hardening the clearest of these first three;
none removes the upper broadband field. These are visual observations, not
proof of intelligibility, healthy phonation or a perceptual ranking.

A supplementary 0.960–1.035 s flow trace, at the same time and vertical scale
for all six full recordings, exposes recurring groups of unequal pulses in
the reference and variants. Strain hardening reduces the largest excursions
and makes that pattern more consistent. Measured-posture feedback lowers the
excursions but leaves a varying pattern. This is more specific than calling
every irregularity random: a repeated multi-pulse structure persists across
different material and feedback hypotheses. It is a candidate contributor to
the reported sickly quality, not an established perceptual cause. This trace
supplements the complete enhanced fields; it does not replace them.

The remaining full fields are also inspected. Shared axial hydraulics (4)
has more ordered fine harmonic structure in the upper second-vowel field than
the fresh reference, but also stronger low-frequency ringing after release.
Measured-posture feedback (5) lowers excursions and releases the main energy
earlier, while broad texture and variable low-band structure remain. The
visual shortlist is 3 and 4, with their limitations preserved; it is not a
perceptual ranking. All six fields contain the complete 72720-sample record
on a 684-by-569 native lattice. Five distinct mono MP3s have been decoded and
checked for full duration, nonzero signal and unclipped playback level.
